## What Is EPANET

EPANET is the U.S. EPA’s water-distribution model. Operators, consultants, and students use it on drinking-water systems they already operate or were hired to study. EPANET water network is the skeleton of junctions and pipes. EPANET hydraulic model is the pressure and flow solution. EPANET water quality is the constituent transport. EPANET distribution system is the whole file: a town, a campus, a district metered area you administer.

It is not AFT Fathom (process liquid, no chlorine age) and not Pipe Flow Expert (shop loops). WaterGEMS and similar commercial GIS seats often wrap or exchange with this engine’s ideas, but the EPANET file is the simple, inspectable model. Calibration against hydrants and SCADA you own is the honest work.

Model only systems you operate, own, or were contracted to analyze. Keep customer data inside the rules of that utility. Do not publish another town’s skeleton as a demo if you do not have permission.

## Starting with EPANET

Prerequisites: A PC you administer, a skeleton of mains you are allowed to model, and demands or SCADA from that system.

1. **Prepare:** Gather pipe diameters, tank curves, and a day of demands from the network you operate.
2. **Configure:** Build EPANET water network with those IDs, not invented street names.
3. **Test:** Run EPANET hydraulic model and compare a few junctions with gauges or hydrant tests you already did.
4. **Refine:** Add EPANET water quality only after hydraulics are believable.
5. **Apply:** Study the scenario the utility asked (closed valve, high day, booster off) on EPANET distribution system.
6. **Maintain:** Archive the `.inp` with the GIS extract date, and do not mix two towns in one file.

## Best Uses

- **Town Or Campus You Operate:** EPANET hydraulic model on mains you administer.
- **Chlorine Age Before A Complaint:** EPANET water quality on that skeleton.
- **Consultant Study You Were Hired For:** Only the system the client delivered.
- **Teaching Lab You Administer:** A network the school already has permission to use.
- **Not A Process Cooling Loop:** Plant liquids belong in Fathom or a pipe sizer.
- **Hydrant Test Calibration:** Adjust roughness on pipes you measured.
- **Tank Mixing As A Scenario:** EPANET distribution system with the tank you actually have.
- **One DMA Per File When That Is How You Operate:** Keep the skeleton readable.
- **Do Not Publish Customer Locations:** Follow the utility’s data rules.
- **Booster Off On A Named Run:** EPANET hydraulic model should include the outage operations asked for.
- **Closed Valve As A Scenario:** EPANET distribution system with the valve ID from the map you keep.
- **Tank Curve From The Site Sheet:** Not a default cylinder if the tank you operate is different.
- **Quality After Hydraulics Only:** EPANET water quality on a skeleton that already matches gauges.
- **Campus Loop Separate From The City File:** EPANET water network should not mix two authorities.
- **Extract Date On The .inp:** So a later GIS dump does not silently replace pipes you calibrated.
